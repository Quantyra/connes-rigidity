\begin{abstract}
We examine J.~L.~Nielsen's argument that applies Ioana's Theorem~8.2
(J.~Amer.\ Math.\ Soc.\ 24 (2011)) to the twisted comultiplication
\[
\Theta(f u_g)=f u_g\otimes\Phi(u_g)
\]
built from an assumed $*$-isomorphism $\Phi\colon L(G)\to L(H)$, in order
to obtain injective group morphisms and run a reductio related to Connes'
rigidity conjecture. We show that this application does not go through: by
construction,
\[
\Theta(A_G)\subseteq A_G\,\bar\otimes\, L(H),
\]
which is the forbidden tensor leg in Ioana's hypothesis~(2). Consequently
Theorem~8.2 does not license the rigid-case output for this map, and the
reductio that depends on that output does not start. This revision corrects our summary of Theorem~8.2 and addresses the three
defenses in Nielsen's 49-page revision, Section~21, and its retained
Section~9 appeal by comparing the required hypotheses. The argument is
supported by primary-source pins of Nielsen NIEWTC and Ioana JAMS~2011, and
by a machine-checked Lean~4 packaging (release
\texttt{v0.1.1-obstruction}) that imports one classical Nielsen
by-construction package and proves semantic hypothesis~(2) failure. Its
non-licensing export is a definitional consequence for a placeholder output,
not a formalization of Ioana's conclusion or injectivity. We do not claim that
Connes' rigidity conjecture is true or false, and we do not validate or
refute the OpenAI or Zhou counterexample constructions.
\end{abstract}

Our previous version overstated Theorem~8.2 by describing its input as unital
and its conclusion as injective group morphisms. Its general input is a
$*$-homomorphism; its torsion-free conclusion gives a homomorphism into the
product group, not asserted injectivity of both coordinates. We correct that

\section{Introduction and non-claims}

\paragraph{Notation.}
$G$ and $H$ are countable discrete groups. $L(G)$ and $L(H)$ are their group
von Neumann algebras. $A_G=L^\infty(X)\rtimes G$ is a Bernoulli crossed product.
$\Phi\colon L(G)\to L(H)$ is an assumed $*$-isomorphism. The twisted
comultiplication is
$\Theta(f u_g)=f u_g\otimes\Phi(u_g)$.
Popa's intertwining-by-bimodules relation is written $\prec$; its negation is
$\nprec$.

Connes' rigidity conjecture (ICC + property~(T) form) asserts: if $G$ and $H$
are countable discrete ICC groups with Kazhdan's property~(T) and
$L(G)\cong L(H)$, then $G\cong H$.

Nielsen proposes a reductio that assumes $L(G)\cong L(H)$, equips $G$ with a
Bernoulli action, forms $A_G$, builds $\Theta$ from $\Phi$, applies Ioana's
classification to extract group morphisms, and derives a contradiction
(Part~I against claimed counterexamples; Part~II as a universal proof of the
conjecture).

\paragraph{In scope.}
Nielsen's use of $\Theta$ with Ioana's Theorem~8.2 to obtain a morphism
$\delta_2$, subsequently argued by Nielsen to be injective---the load-bearing
step of that reductio---and a
machine-checked packaging of the obstruction logic.

\paragraph{Out of scope (explicit non-claims).}
\begin{itemize}
\item Connes rigidity true or false.
\item Validity or invalidity of OpenAI \texttt{ten-proofs} or Zhou
  arXiv:2608.02327 as counterexamples.
\item Full formalization of Ioana~(2011) or of group von Neumann algebras
  $L(\Gamma)$ in mathlib.
\item Lean construction of the spatial tensor product $A_G\bar\otimes L(H)$,
  Bernoulli crossed products, or the W$^*$-closed range of a concrete $\Theta$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Ioana's hypothesis~(2)}

Ioana's Theorem~8.2 takes a general $*$-homomorphism
$\theta\colon N\to M_1\bar\otimes M_2$, in its stated Bernoulli and
relative-property~(T) setting. In the torsion-free case it concludes that
$\theta$ is unital and supplies a character, a group homomorphism
$\delta\colon\Lambda\to\Gamma_1\times\Gamma_2$ and unitary-conjugacy data.
It does not state that both coordinate homomorphisms are injective.
In general it gives a finite-index subgroup $\Lambda_1\subseteq\Lambda$
and a one-to-one map $\delta\colon\Lambda_1\to\Gamma_1\times\Gamma_2$,
with a finite subgroup $K$ normalized by every $\delta(\lambda)$ and
$\delta(\lambda\lambda')^{-1}\delta(\lambda)\delta(\lambda')\in K$;
the accompanying formula has a nonzero corner and finitely many
normalizers of the Cartan algebra. This is not an unqualified pair of
injective coordinate homomorphisms. See \cite[Theorem~8.2,
pp.~1207--1208]{Ioana2011} for the full statement. Nielsen's desired
injectivity of $\delta_2$ is a subsequent claim. Our particular $\Theta$ is
unital, but unitality is not the general input assumption of that theorem.

Both conclusions require the stated hypotheses, including hypothesis~(2):
\begin{align*}
\theta(N)&\nprec_M L(\Gamma_1)\bar\otimes M_2,\\
\theta(N)&\nprec_M M_1\bar\otimes L(\Gamma_2).
\end{align*}
(See Ioana~\cite{Ioana2011}, Theorem~8.2.) Both conjuncts are required. If either
fails, Theorem~8.2 does not license the rigid-case output for that $\theta$.

\section{The obstruction}

\begin{theorem}[Bounded obstruction]
\label{thm:main}
Identify Nielsen's $\Theta\colon A_G\to A_G\bar\otimes A_H$ with Ioana's
$\theta\colon N\to M_1\bar\otimes M_2$ by
$N=A_G$, $M_1=A_G$, $M_2=A_H$, $\Gamma_2=H$.
By Nielsen's definition
\[
\Theta(f u_g)=f u_g\otimes\Phi(u_g),\qquad \Phi(u_g)\in L(H),
\]
one has the containment
\[
\Theta(A_G)\subseteq A_G\,\bar\otimes\, L(H)=M_1\bar\otimes L(\Gamma_2).
\]
If a unital image sits inside a von Neumann subalgebra $B\subseteq M$, then the
image intertwines into $B$ in Popa's sense: in the corner criterion take
both projections equal to $1$, the inclusion homomorphism, and the nonzero
partial isometry $v=1$. The relation $\psi(x)v=vx$ is then immediate
(\cite[Theorem~1.3.1(1), p.~1184]{Ioana2011}; see also \cite{Popa2006}).
Thus one does not have $\theta(N)\nprec_M B$. Taking
$B=M_1\bar\otimes L(\Gamma_2)$ shows that the second conjunct of Ioana's
hypothesis~(2) fails for this $\Theta$. Therefore Theorem~8.2 does not supply
the rigid-case group morphisms from this application. The reductio that requires
$\delta_2$ from that step does not start.
\end{theorem}

\section{Response to the 49-page Nielsen revision}

Here page numbers refer to the 49-page artifact of NIEWTC
\cite{Nielsen2026}, identified by its hash in Section~\ref{sec:sources}.
Definition~8 (p.~21) retains the same formula for $\Theta$ and ambient
$A_G\bar\otimes A_H$. Remark~11 (p.~23; formerly Remark~21 in the
46-page source) and Lemma~14 (pp.~26--27) acknowledge the containment
$\Theta(A_G)\subseteq A_G\bar\otimes L(H)$.
Nielsen does name both mixed targets (p.~24, Case~(II); p.~27) and explicitly
claims to exclude the right one. The issue is that asserted exclusion despite the admitted
containment, not a simple omission of the right target.

\paragraph{The targets are different.}
The single factors $A_G\otimes1$ and $1\otimes A_H$ are distinct from
the left mixed target $L(G)\bar\otimes A_H$ and the right mixed target
$A_G\bar\otimes L(H)$. Lemma~14 invokes Cartan non-intertwining into
$L(G)$, declares the right mixed containment irrelevant to corner
intertwining, and asserts a symmetric exclusion of the right target.
But the corner criterion permits the unit witness just given.
Moreover, $\Theta(L^\infty(X))=L^\infty(X)\otimes1$ lies inside the
right mixed target: the Cartan images are not symmetric between the two
Bernoulli factors. An argument about the left mixed target cannot establish
the required non-intertwining into the right mixed target: it is false here
by the unit witness.

\paragraph{The three defenses in Section~21 (pp.~37--39).}
Remark~13 (pp.~37--38) invokes deformation fixity of $\Theta(u_g)$ and
the Cartan image $L^\infty(X)\otimes1$, uniformly in $\Phi$.
Even granting these properties, they are compatible with the right mixed
containment and do not remove its intertwining witness.
Remark~14 (p.~38) invokes property~(T) spectral gap and describes an
exclusion of intertwining into a single tensor factor. Such an exclusion
does not exclude a mixed target that contains the whole first crossed
product and the second group algebra.
Remark~15 (p.~38) identifies genuine involvement of both factors with the
rigid alternative and refers back to the earlier non-intertwining checks.
Involvement of both factors is compatible with a mixed-target alternative;
it does not establish both conjuncts of hypothesis~(2).

This distinction also matters for a trichotomy formulation. Ioana's
Theorem~C explicitly includes both mixed targets in alternative~(2)
\cite[p.~1179]{Ioana2011}; its own same-factor and torsion-free setting
must also be respected. Invoking internal proof machinery does not eliminate
the disputed premise: the proof of Theorem~8.2 starts under (1)--(2),
uses assumption~(2) to obtain (8.b), and then applies Theorem~6.1
\cite[pp.~1208--1209]{Ioana2011}. The existing containment witness is
therefore still decisive for this particular application. This is
non-licensing by the theorem, not nonexistence of group morphisms or a
claim that its conclusion could never hold for special inputs.

\section{The separate appeal to Ioana's Section~9}

Remark~11 also invokes Theorem~9.1, Lemma~9.2(4), Case~(5), and the
full Section~9 pipeline. This appeal already appeared in the archived
Remark~21; it is not a new October argument. Ioana does treat a mixed
case there, so the Section~8 obstruction should not be presented as an
obstruction to every use of Section~9.

The relevant hypothesis comparison is specific. Theorem~9.1 starts with
a crossed-product isomorphism
\[
 N=C\rtimes\Lambda\ \cong\ qMq,
 \qquad M=B^\Gamma\rtimes\Gamma,
\]
with the actions and groups satisfying its stated assumptions
\cite[Theorem~9.1, p.~1213]{Ioana2011}. The proof transports
comultiplication through this isomorphism before using Lemma~9.2 and
the subsequent cases \cite[pp.~1214--1217]{Ioana2011}.
The group-factor isomorphism $L(G)\cong L(H)$ assumed by Nielsen does
not itself supply this crossed-product-isomorphism setup. Constructing
the injective $\Theta$ is not a construction of that isomorphism, and the
cited appeal supplies no hypothesis-by-hypothesis replacement. Thus the
reviewed appeal demonstrates no repair of the application at issue.
This is a comparison with the published hypotheses, not a new theorem
that such an extension, or any alternative argument, is impossible.

spatial tensor product or the concrete range of $\Theta$. The main exports

hypothesis fails; they do not construct the concrete analytic objects.
The containment bridge is in

hypothesis~(2) failure; it formalizes neither Ioana's conclusion nor injectivity.

\section{Remarks}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Ruled out:} obtaining Ioana Theorem~8.2 rigid-case output from
  this $\Theta$ as defined, and any reductio step that requires that output from
  this application.
\item \textbf{Not ruled out:} Connes' conjecture; independent status of any
  counterexample construction; other theorems of Ioana; other maps in place of
  $\Theta$.
\item \textbf{Lean boundary:} the machine-checked core packages the obstruction
  logic after a classical import of the by-construction containment. It is not a
  full formalization of Ioana~(2011).
\item \textbf{Repair routes (neutral):} a different map, a different theorem, or
  an independent proof that hypothesis~(2) holds for some modified setup. This
  note does not endorse any repair.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

The Nielsen Ioana$+\Theta$ reductio does not go through as an application of
Ioana's Theorem~8.2: the map $\Theta$ is built so that a mandatory
non-intertwining hypothesis fails. Remaining open by design: whether Connes'
conjecture holds, and whether any particular counterexample construction is
correct.
